# Contamination Signatures — Knowledge Base
# Source: Reddit community data (r/ContamFam, r/MushroomGrowers, r/mycology, r/unclebens)
# Scope: Distilled differential signatures for grow-tent contamination diagnosis

---

## M-01: Cobweb Mold (Hypomyces / Cladobotryum)
**Confidence:** High (multiple community confirmations)
**Visual:** Ultra-fluffy, cottony, unstructured growth; greyish-white; lacks the organized "spiky" or "rhizomorphic" structure of healthy mycelium.
**Differential:** Healthy mycelium has controlled, directional spikes and soft tufts. Cobweb is chaotic, amorphous, and grows much faster.
**Action:** Increase FAE, reduce humidity slightly. If confirmed, h2o2 spray (3%) can suppress mild cases.

---

## M-02: Trichoderma (Green Mold)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Bright green patches, often starting as white/cream and turning Emerald green within 48-72h. Powdery texture.
**Differential:** Bruising does not turn green — it turns blue/black and should not expand over time. Trich grows, bruising does not.
**Operational note:** One user applied a **butane lighter burn protocol** to visible trich spots daily for a week+ in a quarantined tub. Tub subsequently pinned and produced a full harvest with only one minor trich return. This is the first documented thermal-remediation success. Not a guarantee, but notable. Early isolation (pre-green) yielded 4 flushes and 600g wet — quarantine upon suspicion is as effective as burn protocol.

---

## M-03: Bacterial Wet Rot / Sour Rot
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Slimy, overly wet grains; yellowing or amber metabolite pooling; sour/acetic smell (distinct from healthy mushroom aroma).
**Differential:** Metabolites ("myc-piss") are amber/dark yellow and pool in discrete spots. Wet rot is uniform sliminess with clumping.
**Action:** No-rinse, no-soak WBS tek or PC at higher moisture ratios. For UB: reduce inoculant volume (<0.5cc), increase GE holes.

---

## M-04: Bruising vs. Contamination Misidentification
**Confidence:** Very High (most common mis-ID)
**Visual:** Bluish, bluish-green, or greyish spots that do NOT expand over 48h. Often appears after break-and-shake, handling, or cold shock.
**Differential:** Contamination grows; bruising is static or fades. Swab test: bruising won't transfer to cotton; mold/trich spores often will (though trich powder may not always).
**Teaching point:** ~60% of "contamination" posts are bruising or metabolite misidentifications.
**Updated discriminator:** If a suspect area shows yellow/green or bluish discoloration and does NOT expand over 3-4 days of observation, it is almost certainly bruising or metabolites — not contamination. The key discriminator is *rate of change*, not color.

---

## M-05: Fuzzy Feet / Overlay from Low FAE
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Dense white fuzz at mushroom bases; can cover entire substrate surface in extreme cases (overlay). Not contamination — stress response.
**Differential:** Cobweb mold is greyish, grows in amorphous clumps, and lacks the dense, cord-like strands of mycelium. Fuzzy feet are white, restricted to base/stem, and correlate with poor air exchange.
**Action:** Increase passive or active FAE. If overlay is severe, fork-tek or bubble-tek to re-expose surface for pinning.

---

## M-06: Black Debris in Liquid Culture (Endospore vs. Particulate)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Black or dark particulate floating in LC solution; may settle or suspend.
**Differential:** True mold/black contamination should grow or expand in LC. Inert debris (carbonized grain, charcoal from filter, etc.) will remain static. Post-break-and-shake, debris often redistributes.
**Action:** When in doubt, transfer to agar for isolation rather than grain directly.

---

## M-07: Powdery Coating on Caps (Second Flush)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** White/grey powdery deposit on mushroom caps, especially second flush.
**Differential:** Could be aerial mycelium, spore deposit from prior flush, mild cobweb residue, or leftover substrate particles. If it wipes off easily and caps are firm, likely benign. If fuzzy and spreading, suspect cobweb.

---

## M-08: Ring / Circle Formation in Monotub
**Confidence:** Low (unresolved)
**Visual:** Perfect or near-perfect circular ring in substrate; pins forming inside or around it.
**Differential:** Could be localized bacterial contamination creating a nutrient gradient, metabolite concentration ring, or physical damage/imperfection in substrate packing.
**Teaching point:** Rings/circles are often dismissed but can indicate hidden bacterial pockets or uneven hydration.

---

## M-09: Grey Top-Layer Contamination in Long-Colonized Jars
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Top layer of grain jar (2+ months old) turns slightly grey. Mycelium below may be healthy.
**Differential:** This is likely delayed bacterial takeover or dried-out mycelium senescence, not mold.
**Action:** If below the surface is healthy white and firm, consider S2B with surface layer discarded.

---

## M-10: Insect/Pest Contamination (Fungus Gnats)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Tiny flies emerging from tub when opened; larvae may be visible in substrate.
**Cause:** Over-field-capacity substrate, standing water, or organic debris. Open-air fruiting without fine mesh.
**Action:** Ensure no standing water in tent. Draining and careful FAE filter maintenance are preventive. Yellow sticky traps near intakes.

---

## M-11: Yellowing / Amber Metabolites ("Myc-Piss")
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Yellow or amber liquid pooling on substrate surface or in grain jars.
**Differential:** Metabolites signal mycelial stress (over-hydration, bacterial competition, temperature swings) but are NOT inherently contamination. If accompanied by sour smell -> bacterial.
**Action:** Evaluate environmental stressors (humidity, temperature, FAE). If isolated and no smell, often acceptable.

---

## M-12: Fridge Storage Discoloration
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Cold-stored fully colonized grain jars show discoloration after retrieval.
**Differential:** Cold bruising from temperature shock vs. latent bacterial growth during slow metabolism in cold.
**Action:** Let jars warm to room temp overnight. Inspect for green/odd growth. If uniform grey/blue and mycelium is firm -> likely bruising.

---

## M-13: Ashen / Grey Grain Surface (Bacterial + Possible Aspergillus)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Top layer or upper half of grain jar turns ashen grey to black; looks like ash or powder coating grain. Bottom may show semi-healthy mycelium + amber metabolite pooling.
**Differential:** Composite failure — bacterial wet rot at the surface degrading grain, combined with powdery mold colonizing the compromised tissue. "Window lickers" — patches where mycelium reaches but fails to fully colonize — as a tell for bacterial undergrowth beneath the ash.
**Teaching point:** The ash-grey is a warning of multiple simultaneous invaders. If you see "window lickers" alongside discoloration, it's bacterial rot with mold opportunism.
**Action:** Dispose. Not recoverable.

---

## M-14: Post-Dunk Fuzzy Regrowth (Healthy Mycelial Reaction)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** After 24h dunk between flushes, substrate surface develops white fuzzy growth that looks like cobweb or overlay.
**Differential:** Normal mycelial fuzz following rehydration. Key discriminator: uniform white, does not spread rapidly into amorphous clumps, distilled water + proper dunk protocol. Cobweb mold would appear more greyish, grow in chaotic directions, and often emerge without a wet trigger.
**Teaching point:** Rehydration triggers a mycelial response. White uniformity + known dunk trigger + no color change -> healthy. Grey, amorphous, rapid expansion without a wet trigger -> suspect cobweb.

---

## M-15: Long-Term Sealed LC Viability
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Liquid culture in sealed container left years in storage. Visually clear with no visible contam.
**Differential:** The absence of visible contamination does not equal health. Extended dormancy (>2 years) leads to genetic senescence.
**Action:** Label and date all LCs. Culture age >12 months should be treated as suspect for senescence even if visually clean. Revive on agar first, not direct-to-grain.

---

## M-16: Environmental Failure — Space Heater / Dry Heat
**Confidence:** High (multiple independent reports)
**Visual:** Bald patches on substrate, stalled pins or full aborts at ~1/3-1/2 growth, caps turning dark.
**Context:** Space heaters rapidly dry the local environment and create uneven temperature/humidity gradients. The symptom is not contamination but environmental collapse.
**Differential:** True trich or bacterial contamination would show localized color change (green, yellow) and grow outward. Space-heater damage is diffuse, uniform, and correlates with heater run-time.
**Action:** Monitor dew-point, not just temperature. If using a heater, add humidity source (ultrasonic humidifier). Position heater so it does not blow directly across tubs.

---

## M-17: LC Over-Colonization (Dense Mycelial Clumping)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** In a 2+ month-old LC jar, clear or slightly translucent white "spots" or lumps appear floating inside the liquid and on the surface.
**Differential:** Dense mycelial aggregation, not contamination. Key discriminator: the LC was known-good, the spots are uniformly white and non-fuzzy.
**Action:** Gentle swirl or stir before drawing. If white, firm, and smells like mushrooms (not sour) -> over-colonized, not contaminated.

---

## M-18: Early-Stage Trichoderma (Bright White Before Green)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** A very bright, almost luminous white patch that appears suddenly — "popped up overnight." Denser and more opaque than surrounding healthy mycelium.
**Differential:** Early trichoderma mycelium is bright white for the first 24-48h before sporulating green. Key warnings: (1) overnight appearance, (2) brighter/denser than adjacent healthy mycelium, (3) slightly raised texture.
**Action:** Quarantine immediately. Monitor for greening within 48h. Do not open near other grows.

---

## M-19: Orange / Brown Stem Lines (Bruising Variant)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Brown or rust-colored lines running vertically from cap down the stipe. Uniform coloration, linear rather than blotchy.
**Differential:** Often genetic in certain strains (B+, Avery's Albino, etc.) or bruising after handling. Not bacterial blotch.
**Action:** If uniform, linear, dry, and non-spreading -> ignore. If wet, spreading, yellow/amber, or accompanied by smell -> investigate bacterial blotch (M-03).

---

## M-20: Pre-Visual Bacterial Detection (Sour Smell)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Sensory:** Sour, acetic, or "off" smell emanating from tub or jar with **no visible discoloration or growth** yet.
**Differential:** Healthy mycelium smells earthy, mushroom-y, or neutral. A sour smell before any visual sign is almost always bacterial colonization at early stage.
**Teaching point:** When sensor logs are available, sour-smell reports correlate with humidity saturation >98% for >2h and CO2 re-accumulation shape changes 24-48h prior. The nose is an early-warning system.
**Action:** Immediate environmental audit — check for standing water, over-field-capacity substrate, blocked FAE, or temperature swings.

---

## M-21: Post-Harvest Drying Failure (Black Spots / Enzymatic Browning)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Mushroom caps or stems develop black, mushy spots during air-drying.
**Differential:** Not contamination. Moisture retention causing enzymatic browning and bacterial proliferation post-harvest.
**Action:** Invest in a dehydrator. Air-drying in humid climates is unreliable. Target cracker-dry within 24h of harvest.

---

## M-22: Over-Inoculation Failure Mode (Swampy Bags)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Slow or stalled colonization; excessive condensation; "swampy" environment inside bag.
**Context:** Injecting full 10mL spore syringe into a 0.75lb all-in-one bag creates free-water saturation.
**Teaching point:** More inoculant != faster colonization. For UB/all-in-one bags, 0.5-1.0cc is sufficient.
**Action:** Standardize volumes. .75lb bags -> 0.5cc max. 3lb bags -> 1.0-1.5cc.

---

## M-23: White Blobs in LC That Do Not Break With Shaking
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Opaque white blobs floating on LC surface. Gentle shaking does **not** break them up.
**Differential:** M-17 (over-colonization) usually breaks apart when swirled. These blobs are opaque, surface-bound, and shake-resistant — possibly early bacterial biofilm, yeast colonies, or a microbial pellicle.
**Action:** Agitate vigorously. If they persist -> transfer to agar. Do not use for grain inoculation until confirmed clean.

---

## M-24: Post-Dunk Bacterial Contamination (Flush #1 -> #2 Transition)
**Confidence:** High (reinforcing pattern, n>=3)
**Visual:** Contamination signs appearing within 24-72h after a full submerged dunk between flushes.
**Differential:** Post-dunk healthy mycelial fuzz (M-14) is uniform white. Post-dunk bacterial contamination is patchy, yellow/amber, may smell sour.
**Teaching point:** Tap water with chlorine is safer than distilled for bacterial suppression. Ensure complete drainage.
**Action:** If dunk water is standing after drain-off, gently tilt tub and absorb. Consider hydrated lime (calcium hydroxide) at 1g/L in dunk water to raise pH.

---

## M-25: Lion's Mane Contamination — Green/Blue/Yellow Mold on Blocks
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Lion's Mane grow blocks showing colored mold patches — often starting at the filter patch, puncture wounds, or along block edges.
**Differential:** Lion's Mane mycelium is naturally fluffy, white, and tends to form icicle-like primordia when healthy. Any colored patch on a LM block is almost certainly contamination.
**Action:** Inspect filter patches daily after the second flush return. LM mycelium is aggressive and can outcompete minor bacterial contamination, but once trich or penicillium takes hold, it spreads fast due to LM's fluffy, low-density mycelium at the surface.

---

## M-26: NSS (No-Soak-No-Simmer) Brown Rice Spawn Clumping
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Brown rice spawn, prepared via NSS method, clumps into a solid mass after PC'ing. Shake fails to break the clump.
**Differential:** Dense, anaerobic center of the clump is prime bacterial territory — especially bacillus.
**Teaching point:** Brown rice starch gelatinizes during PC and glues grains together. WBS or millet with varying particle sizes is far better suited to NSS.
**Action:** For brown rice, always soak 12-24h and drain thoroughly before PC. Avoid NSS for brown rice entirely.

---

## M-27: Pins Growing on Non-Substrate Surfaces (Plastic / Bag Walls)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Mushroom pins forming on plastic liner, tub walls, or side of a brick — not the intended substrate surface.
**Context:** Not contamination but indicates the substrate surface is too dry, compacted, or micro-climate is unfavorable.
**Action:** Increase surface humidity or fork-tek to create micro-crevices for pinning.

---

## M-28: PE (Penis Envy) Blue Cap Bruising
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** PE strain mushrooms with vibrant blue coloration on caps, often appearing "overnight."
**Differential:** PE bruises more aggressively than B+ or Golden Teachers. Blue caps on PE are normal. Only act if blue is accompanied by soft/mushy texture, wet spots, or off smell.

---

## M-29: Bright White "Popped" Patches (Early Trichoderma / Senescent Mycelium)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** A very bright, dense white patch on otherwise healthy colonized substrate that "pops up" locally within 10 days post-spawn.
**Differential:** Early trich tends to expand radially; healthy rebounds consolidate into pins.
**Action:** Photograph at 12h intervals. If it expands outward radially, turns green, or develops powdery texture -> quarantine per M-02. If it consolidates into hyphal knots or pins -> healthy.

---

## M-30: Mycogone / Wet Bubble Disease (Mycogone perniciosa)
**Confidence:** Medium (first case in corpus)
**Visual:** White-to-cream soft blobs growing alongside or on top of normal fruit bodies. Malformed mass of mycelium and tissue. Watery blisters may be present.
**Differential:** NOT metabolites (M-11) — metabolites are amber/yellow liquid. NOT cobweb (M-01) — cobweb is grey amorphous fuzz. Mycogone presents as firm, tissue-like blobs with stoppered-gill formation.
**Action:** Remove infected tissue with a sterile spoon. Improve FAE and reduce humidity slightly. Some growers report success with salt-burial on the affected spot.

---

## M-31: Pink / Red Agar Contamination (Rhodotorula / Fusarium / Airborne Contaminant)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Distinct pink, red, or salmon-colored growth ring or patch on agar. Appears 2-4 days after transfer.
**Differential:** Pink/red on agar is almost NEVER healthy mushroom mycelium. Common candidates: *Rhodotorula* yeast, *Fusarium*, Serratia marcescens bacterium, Neurospora.
**Action:** Discard the plate. Review sterile technique — especially proximity to sinks (Serratia risk) and UV-PC timing for plates.

---

## M-32: Spontaneous Mycelial Growth on Stored Coir (Environmental Opportunism)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Plain, unused coco coir left in storage develops white mycelial growth from airborne spores.
**Teaching point:** Even "sterile-looking" environments harbor fungal spores. Demonstrates why open storage of substrate invites contamination.
**Action:** If uniform white with no green/yellow/black patches, likely a benign saprophyte. Not recommended for controlled grows.

---

## M-33: Black Mold in Bulk Substrate Bags
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Black, irregular patches on or within substrate of a mushroom grow bag. Substrate contaminated before or during the mix.
**Differential:** Pre-existing substrate contamination — not introduced post-inoculation. Could be *Aspergillus niger* or a dark bacterial colony.
**Action:** Do not use questionable substrate batches. Test a small test bag from each batch before scaling.

---

## M-34: LC Pooling / Free-Water Accumulation at Bottom of Jar
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Clear pooled water at the bottom of a grain spawn jar immediately after inoculation with liquid culture.
**Mechanism:** Grain at field-capacity pushed past saturation by additional LC volume. Excess pools at bottom creating anaerobic, bacterial-friendly environment.
**Action:** Reduce LC volume. Pint jars: 1-1.5mL max. Quart jars: 2mL max. If pooling occurs, lie jar on side with filter up.
**Teaching point:** "Shake after inoculation" won't eliminate free water — it only redistributes it. Lying the jar on its side increases surface area and prevents pooling.

---

## M-35: Mycelium Outcompeting Trichoderma (Aggressive Strain / Rare Repairable Case)
**Confidence:** Medium (anecdotal)
**Visual:** Green trichoderma spot(s) present but mycelium continues to advance and cover them.
**Differential:** Mycelium advancing *over* the green spot, not retreating. If green spot growing larger -> standard trich. If stable or shrinking -> possible genetic outcompetition.
**Action:** Quarantine and monitor. Do NOT open in grow room. Clone the aggressive isolate — it may have value if trich resistance is repeatable.

---

## M-36: Cereal Grain Mycelium Growth Patterns (Fresh Spawn on Dry Substrate)
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Cereal grain jars with thin, wispy mycelium that appears "different" from prior grows.
**Differential:** Rye produces a "frosting" effect — thin white film over grain rather than thick ropy cords. Cobweb on grain tends to be greyish and grows faster.
**Action:** Compare growth rate (mm/day). Healthy rye: 1-2mm/day. Cobweb: 5-10mm/day. h2o2 test: cobweb fizzes immediately; healthy mycelium does not.

---

## M-37: Refracted Condensation / Moisture Rings (Aesthetic Confusion)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Condensation on the lid creates a "halo" or bright ring effect when viewed from above.
**Differential:** True mycelium or cobweb is on the substrate surface, not on the lid.
**Action:** Verify whether a suspect patch is on the substrate surface or on the container wall. Camera flash and lid condensation create frequent false alarms in first 24-48h.

---

## M-38: Pre-Sterile Grain Substrate Vendor Failures (Batch Contamination)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Multiple bags from the same vendor showing identical contamination patterns despite clean technique.
**Differential:** Single-user technique failure vs. vendor mass contamination.
**Action:** Test vendor substrate with a control (water-only inoculation). Maintain a vendor contamination database.
**Teaching point:** "Pre-sterile" substrate is only as reliable as the vendor's sterilization and packaging.

---

## M-39: Composite Multi-Color Contamination (Uncolonized Grain Dump)
**Confidence:** High
**Visual:** Multiple simultaneous contamination colors — green, blue, yellow, orange — from deliberately mixed uncolonized grain.
**Differential:** Multiple colors appear simultaneously rather than sequentially. Substrate compromised at multiple points.
**Teaching point:** Uncolonized grain is an open buffet. Spawn-to-bulk requires fully colonized grain.
**Action:** Dispose. Not recoverable.

---

## M-40: Grow Room Ambient Spore Load / Post-Trich Room Contamination
**Confidence:** Medium
**Context:** A grow room where a fully contaminated tub has been sitting can accumulate airborne spores. Trichoderma spores are fine, lightweight, and remain viable on surfaces and in air circulation for weeks.
**Action:** After any confirmed trich contamination:
1. Remove and dispose of contaminated substrate.
2. Deep-clean all surfaces with 10% bleach solution.
3. Run HEPA filtration for 24-48h if available.
4. Avoid introducing new spawn for 1-2 weeks.
5. Consider an agar "air test" (exposed plate test) before reintroduction.

---

## M-41: Black Soot / Particulate in Pre-Packaged AIO Bags
**Confidence:** Medium
**Visual:** Black or dark particulate material inside a pre-packaged all-in-one grow bag.
**Differential:** Manufacturing debris vs. early-stage Aspergillus niger.
**Action:** Document with photo, contact vendor. If earthy/neutral smell and dry/gritty -> likely debris. If musty or moldy smell -> discard.

---

## M-42: Delayed Colonization (2+ Months Dormant -> Recovery)
**Confidence:** High
**Context:** Substrate injected months ago with no visible growth, then subjected to B&S and moved to warmer location. Growth subsequently appeared.
**Differential:** Not contamination — extreme delayed colonization. Spores or LC can remain viable for months.
**Teaching point:** Mycelium is resilient. 2 months of dormancy does not equal substrate death if the spawn was viable. Temperature is the dominant trigger.
**Action:** Monitor for normal mycelial progression. If white, healthy, and growing outward -> proceed as normal.
