# Cultivation Techniques

## Sustainability Metrics
- Water efficiency: 6.8L water per 0.5kg mushrooms — extremely low water footprint compared to other crops
- Energy efficiency: 1.0 kWh electricity per 0.5kg batch — minimal electricity requirement
- Sustainability positioning: Mushroom cultivation highly sustainable for marketing

## Alternative Protein Applications
- King oyster mushrooms as meat substitute: texture works for pulled "pork" sandwiches, sauteed, stir-fried, grilled applications
- Commercial validation: MyBacon (mycelium-based bacon) named TIME Best Invention 2022 — mycelium food products gaining mainstream recognition

## Research Resources & Opportunities
- **Free DNA sequencing available**: US/Canada regions eligible for unlimited mushroom DNA sequencing via MycoMap Network — citizen science opportunity for strain identification and cataloging
- **Mycorrhiza research volume**: 1000+ mycorrhizal research papers published annually (2026) — field experiencing rapid growth, worth monitoring for cultivation insights

## Species-Specific Substrate Performance
- **Lion's Mane on grain**: Poor performance confirmed on brown rice/vermiculite cakes — validates hardwood substrate preference for Hericium species

## Fruiting Vessel Patterns
- **All-in-one bag stall pattern**: Common failure mode where colonized bags transferred to tubs show no fruiting after 1+ month — requires casing layer, improved FAE, or extended patience beyond standard timelines

## Slime Mold Cultivation & Biology
- **Physarum sclerotium formation indicators**: Orange coloration + stopped movement indicates entry into dormant survival state (sclerotium) or sporulation — reactivation protocol: transfer to fresh agar, darkness, paper drying for preservation

## Mycorrhizal Ecology
- **Mycorrhizal residence time dynamics**: Research shows residence time shapes fungal partners of alien trees — longer residence = more established mycorrhizal networks. Implication: fungal adaptation to host plants follows temporal patterns similar to network maturation

## Substrate Preparation Patterns
- **Substrate packing density**: Loose pack in monotub slows colonization — proper tamping/pressing of substrate improves colonization speed and uniformity
- **Lion's Mane grain spawn**: Can fruit prematurely on uncolonized grain under stress (light, temperature) — monitor for early pinning as stress indicator before substrate introduction

## Sterilization Protocols
- **Pressure cooker sterilization parameters**: 121°C (250°F) at 15 PSI for 90 minutes for supplemented hardwood substrates and grain spawn
- **Pressure-temperature relationship**: 15 PSI required to reach 121°C (water boils at 100°C at 0 PSI regardless of time/heat)
- **Post-sterilization cooling critical**: Must cool to below 30°C before inoculation — heat will kill spawn
- **Safety protocol**: Never leave pressure cooker unattended during operation
- **Wood-decomposer category**: Lion's Mane, Shiitake, Oyster, Turkey Tail require full sterilization (121°C/15 PSI) — pasteurization (150-180°F) insufficient for these species
- **Timing planning**: Inoculation same day possible if sterilizing morning (cooling takes several hours)

## Fruiting Timeline Strategies
- **Pre-spawned mycelium procurement**: Using pre-colonized spawn reduces time-to-fruiting from months to weeks — viable strategy for accelerating first grow cycle validation

## Low-Tech & DIY Methods
- **Wild wood-lover cloning without agar (cardboard tek):** Pasteurized cardboard successfully colonized by wild wood-loving mushroom tissue. Growth slows as cardboard dries; rehydration with distilled water can restart growth. Cardboard substrate works as a low-tech agar substitute for species that colonize lignin/cellulose. Limitations: slower than agar, harder to isolate clean sectors, contamination risk higher without visual clarity. Liquid culture from cardboard-grown mycelium is viable but requires sterile technique.
- **IKEA TRIXIG floor protectors as jar filters:** Furniture felt pads (IKEA TRIXIG) used as 1/4in (6mm) hole jar filters. Reported contamination-free over ~1 year. Advantages: inexpensive, widely available, no specialized ordering. Process: stick pad to lid exterior over hole, PC with grains. Validated as functional filter material comparable to synthetic filter discs.

## Strain-Specific Ecology & Notes
- **Shipwreck strain FAE sensitivity:** "Shipwreck" (active strain, possibly NSS-related) exhibits extreme aerial mycelium / fuzzy feet when FAE insufficient. Even tubs with "lots of gaps" may not provide enough passive exchange. Community consensus: needs modified shoebox with flipped lid or removed patches. Strain-specific behavior: thrives in high-airflow conditions where other strains might dry out.
- **Antler reishi spore drop + condensation pattern:** Brownish coloration on developing antler reishi after a few days is *Ganoderma* spore drop, not contamination. However, condensation pattern (moisture vapor rising, condensing on bag sides, dripping onto substrate) simultaneously generates metabolites and creates anaerobic pockets.
- **Filter effectiveness for indoor spore mitigation:** HEPA-13 filters (0.3 micron) theoretically block mushroom spores (~3+ microns). MERV13 on duct exhaust also used. Indoor grow risk assessment: spores already present in ambient air; active growth without underlying moisture/mold issues is low risk.
- **Burn morel ecology:** Post-wildfire "burn zone gold" — *Morchella* species fruit prolifically in recent burn areas. Mechanism: fire clears competition, releases nutrients, creates ash-based pH shift. Seasonal timing critical: typically first spring after fire.
- **No-rinse WBS tek:** Wild bird seed spawn prep without rinse, soak, or simmer. Add dry WBS to jar (~half full, ~400 mL), add water to grain surface level (2:1 WBS:water by weight), PC. Reported to hit field capacity perfectly with minimal effort.
- **Honey water dunk speculation (5% for second flush):** Hypothesized technique using 5% honey water as dunk medium for extra growth and bacterial inhibition during second flush. Community response cautious; no controlled data. Currently speculative.
- **Millet spawn prep failure modes:** Recurring problem of overcooked millet (mushy or burnt) or under-hydration. Community sharing no-soak millet protocols as alternative. Suggests millet hydration window is narrower than other grains (popcorn, WBS).
- **Refrigerating fresh mushrooms for delayed cloning:** Fresh mushrooms can be refrigerated (whole, not sliced) for several days while awaiting agar supplies. Internal tissue remains viable for cloning if kept cold and dry.
- **Outdoor disposal of spent spawn — Tampanensis resilience:** Spent or contaminated grain spawn and substrate folded into veggie beds produced outdoor *Psilocybe tampanensis* fruits. Demonstrates sclerotia-forming species can persist and fruit in outdoor garden disposal scenarios. Limited to shaded microclimates.
- **Lion's Mane premature fruiting on blocks:** Gourmet blocks fruiting early before full colonization reported. Suggests either strain-specific early-pinning behavior or environmental trigger (light, temperature). Relevant to Hericium block timing protocols.
- **Semi-autonomous gourmet mushroom greenhouse:** Hobbyist builds showing small automated greenhouse for gourmet mushrooms. Automation interest cross-pollinating between mycology and maker communities.
