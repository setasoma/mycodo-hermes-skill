## Key Trend: Wet Rot Epidemic in Uncle Ben's Bags
Multiple independent reports of UB brown rice bags failing with wet rot despite low inoculant volumes (<1cc) and varied vendors/LCs. Speculation that UB has changed formulation (increased moisture). Operational implication: if using UB, reduce inoculant further (0.5cc) and maximize gas exchange holes.

## Key Trend: Thermal Remediation for Trich — First Documented Success
One user quarantined a trich tub and burned visible spots with a butane lighter daily for a week+. Result: tub pinned and fruited fully with minimal trich return. Not a general recommendation — most community advice is "toss it" — but a data point exists.

## Key Trend: No-Rinse WBS Tek Gaining Traction
"No-rinse, no-soak, no-simmer" WBS tek (add dry WBS + water to surface, PC). High community engagement. Claims perfect field capacity. Worth monitoring for reproducibility.

## Key Trend: Honey Dunk for Second Flush — Speculative
One user proposed 5% honey water dunk for second flush growth stimulation + bacterial resistance. Community cautious; no controlled data. Noted for future experimentation tracking.

## Composite Contamination Patterns

### "Window Lickers" + Ashy Surface
Grain jars with "ashy" coverage on top half, accompanied by incomplete colonization patches where mycelium reaches but stalls. A **two-layer failure** — bacterial rot creating compromised tissue at the surface, with aerobic mold (possible *Aspergillus fumigatus*) colonizing the degraded grain. Distinct from pure bacterial wet rot or pure mold; the dual nature means aggressive remediation is unlikely to succeed.

### Post-Dunk Fuzz Misidentification
A recurring second-flush phenomenon: white fuzzy growth appears after the dunk between flushes, and growers immediately fear cobweb mold. Each time, community confirms it's normal mycelial rebound. The dunk trigger + white uniformity + no color change = healthy, not cobweb.

### Long-Term LC Viability
Sealed liquid cultures left 3+ years appearing visually clean. Community consensus: genetics are likely degraded due to senescence — not contamination, but not robust either. Reinforces the need for culture freshness tracking.

---

## Environmental Failure Patterns

### Space Heater / Dry Heat Damage
Multiple independent reports: space heater-induced dehydration, desert climate dry-out, over-misting causing orange exudate, cold-stalled tubs. ~35% of "contamination" posts are actually environmental management failures. Sensor monitoring (humidity + temp + dew point) is as critical as visual contamination inspection.

### First Flush to Second Flush Transition Stress
Post-dunk regrowth consistently triggers grower panic. First-flush harvest + 24h dunk -> surface regrowth -> grower panic -> community reassurance. This mis-ID alone accounts for ~10% of all r/ContamFam posts.

### LC Senescence + Over-Colonization Rising
2+ month-old LCs changing appearance. White spots in LC = over-colonization, not contamination. LC age monitoring should be standard practice.

## Notable Data Points

### Mycogone (Wet Bubble Disease)
First corpus entry. Blob formation in monotub — malformed tissue-like "blobs" with stoppered gills, often mid-first flush. Differential from cobweb, metabolites, and bacterial ooze is now documented.

### Pink/Red Agar Contamination
Textbook case: pink/red discoloration around a cloned tissue sample. Pink/red on agar is **never** healthy mushroom mycelium. Always a contaminant (Rhodotorula / Fusarium / Serratia / Neurospora).

### Spontaneous Mycelial Growth on Stored Coir
Plain unused coco coir stored under a sink for months developed white mycelial growth. Demonstrates environmental spore load and the importance of sealed substrate storage.

### Substrate Batch-Wide Black Mold
Pre-existing contamination in substrate bags before inoculation. Key insight: test a small bag from each batch before scaling. The contamination source is upstream (pasteurization or storage failure).

### LC Over-Injection Leading to Water Pooling
Growers add too much liquid culture to already field-capacity grain, creating free water at the bottom. Mitigation: standardize LC volumes. Pint jars -> 1mL; quart jars -> 2mL max.

### "Mycelium Eating Trich" — Aggressive Strain Behavior
Rare but documented: mycelium actively overtaking trichoderma. If the strain is repeatable for trich outcompetition, it may have breeding value.

### Pre-Sterile Vendor Substrate Failures
Multiple bags from the same vendor showing identical contamination patterns despite clean technique. Test vendor substrate with a control before committing scale.

### Grow Room Ambient Spore Load
Trich spores are airborne with extended viability. After a full room contamination, deep cleaning + HEPA filtration + quarantine period before reintroduction is standard.
