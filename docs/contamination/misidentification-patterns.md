# Common Contamination Misidentifications

Quick-reference for the highest-frequency false-positive patterns seen in mushroom cultivation communities. When classifying a suspected contamination, scan this list before concluding pathogen.

---

## M-ID-01: Yellow Mycelium / "Mycelium Piss"

**Frequency:** ~15% of contamination posts

**Reality:** Hydration stress or metabolite production. NOT bacterial or mold contamination.

**Differential:** Contamination spreads rapidly with defined edges; yellow mycelium is diffuse and substrate-wide. Metabolites bead up like dew; bacterial film spreads flat and continuous.

**Context trigger:** Dry substrate conditions, low humidity spikes.

---

## M-ID-02: Bruising vs Trichoderma

**Frequency:** ~30% of all contamination posts (highest single category)

**Tests:**
- **Q-tip test:** Rub suspected area. Bruising transfers color to swab, contamination does not.
- **Texture test:** Bruising is flat and surface-level; Trichoderma produces fuzzy/hairy texture that rises above substrate.
- **Spread test:** Bruising appears suddenly after handling/harvest; Trichoderma spreads over 24-48h with visible expansion.

---

## M-ID-03: Fuzzy Feet (Low FAE) vs Cobweb Mold

**Frequency:** ~12% of contamination posts

- **Fuzzy feet:** White, attached to base of pins/stems, uniformly colored, does NOT spread across substrate surface. Resolved by increased FAE within 24-48h.
- **Cobweb (Dactylium/Cladobotrym):** Greyish, rises above substrate in wispy threads, spreads rapidly overnight. Has chaotic, uncontrolled appearance.

**Note:** Copelandia mycelium is naturally fluffier than Pleurotus or Psilocybe -- higher false-positive rate for cobweb. Look for organized directional edges even when fluffy.

---

## M-ID-04: Metabolites vs Bacterial Infection

**Frequency:** ~8% of contamination posts

- **Metabolites:** Yellow/orange/brown liquid droplets, often around uncolonized patches. Natural byproduct.
- **Bacterial:** Slimy, spreading film with defined edges; may have sour odor; pools rather than beads.

---

## M-ID-05: Cold Bruising on Stored Spawn

**Frequency:** Seasonal (~3% in colder months)

Grey/blue discoloration on fully colonized grain after fridge storage. Normal if temps stayed above freezing. No fuzzy growth, no off-odors, uniform discoloration.

---

## M-ID-06: Powdery Cap Coating (2nd Flush)

**Frequency:** ~5% of second-flush posts

White/grey powder on mushroom caps during second flush. Often spore residue from first flush or environmental sporulation. Spore residue wipes off; mold penetrates tissue and has fuzzy margin.

---

## M-ID-07: Brown Stipe Lines / Shadows

Shadows from natural indentations/veins on stipe surface, especially visible when fruit lies on its side. Lines disappear when stem is handled (not actual material).

---

## M-ID-08: Dark Liquid Culture Sediment

**Frequency:** ~4% of LC-related posts

Black/dark specks in aged LC jars. Often settled grain particulates from LC preparation, NOT mold. Grain sediment is evenly distributed, static, non-fuzzy; mold shows growth over time with fuzzy edges.

**Confirmation test:** Inoculate grain with specked LC. If colonization proceeds normally, sediment is harmless.

---

## M-ID-09: Albino Phenotypic Variation (Yellow Pins)

Strains: Jack Frost, APE variants, other albino lines.

Yellow or differently colored pins emerging alongside white pins. Normal phenotypic expression in albino strains. Yellow pins with healthy morphology, growing in synchrony with white pins, no spreading or fuzzy halo.

---

## M-ID-10: Wet Rot + Surface Mycelium Overgrowth

**Frequency:** ~6% of grain-spawn posts

White mycelium visibly growing over wet-rotted grain sections. The false conclusion is that mycelium is "beating" the contamination. Reality: Bacillus load remains in grain interior. At spawn-to-bulk, bacteria proliferate across entire substrate regardless of surface coverage.

**Rule:** Discard ANY grain with wet rot signs. Do not section-and-salvage. Do not trust surface mycelium as victory.

---

## M-ID-11: Over-Sterilization Discoloration

**Frequency:** ~3% of technique posts

Grains appear burnt, darkened, or overcooked after pressure cooking. Over-sterilization degrades grain integrity, creating anaerobic zones where bacteria establish before mycelium can take hold.

**Critical parameters:** 121C, 15 PSI, correct duration (varies by grain type). Millet requires tighter water ratio and shorter simmer than whole brown rice.

---

## M-ID-12: Pink/Red Fuzzy Feet

Normally fuzzy feet are uniformly white. Pink, red, or orange tinge on fuzzy growth indicates secondary bacterial infection (Pseudomonas, Serratia) rather than benign aerial mycelium.

---

## M-ID-13: Pink/Red on Agar

Pink/red on agar is NEVER healthy mushroom mycelium. Common candidates: Rhodotorula yeast (pink, slimy film), Fusarium (pink-to-red, cottony), Serratia marcescens (red spots, often near sinks), Neurospora (pink/orange, fast-growing).

**Context trigger:** New growers on first or second plate; often stored near kitchen or bathroom sink.

---

## M-ID-14: Bright White "Popped" Spot on Colonized Substrate

**Frequency:** ~3% of posts

Two possibilities:
1. **Vigorous mycelium rebound** -- non-expanding, isolated, associated with hyphal knot formation. Consolidates into pins.
2. **Early Trichoderma** -- expands radially over 24-48h, brighter and denser than adjacent mycelium.

**Differential:** Healthy bright spots are static or consolidate into knots; early trich expands outward, turns green within 48-72h, develops slightly raised/puffy texture.

**Action:** Photograph at 12h intervals. Radial expansion + greening = quarantine immediately. Consolidation into pins = healthy.

---

## M-ID-15: Mycogone / Wet Bubble Disease

White-to-cream soft "blobs" or malformed masses growing on or alongside normal fruit bodies, sometimes with watery blistering underneath. Not early pins (pins form at substrate surface with organized gill development), not bacterial ooze (ooze is liquid and amber), not cobweb (cobweb is grey, wispy, surface-spreading).

**Action:** Remove infected tissue with sterile spoon. Improve FAE and reduce humidity slightly. Cull substrate if spread continues.
