# Contamination Remediation Techniques

Documented non-destructive or minimally-destructive interventions that have produced recovery in mushroom cultivation. Source: community reports and operator experiments.

---

## General Principle: Quarantine > Disinfection > Salvage

Hierarchy of response when contamination is detected:

1. **Quarantine** -- isolate from other grows immediately (spores travel on air, hands, tools)
2. **Identify** -- determine if surface or deep, bacterial or fungal, early or advanced
3. **Disinfect** if surface and early -- H2O2, thermal, or excision
4. **Salvage** if intervention succeeds -- monitor for re-emergence
5. **Dispose** if deep, advanced, or recurrent -- compost outside, do not open indoors

---

## R-01: Spatial Isolation (Strongest Evidence)

**Technique:** At first suspicion of contamination, immediately remove the fruiting chamber from the grow area and continue fruiting in isolation (separate room, sealed container, or distant shelf).

**Documented result:** Early greenish suspicion on day prior to pinning. Tub immediately quarantined to separate room. Tub produced 4 subsequent flushes yielding approximately 600g wet total. Definitive green sporulation only appeared after the 4th flush.

**Caveats:**
- Requires distinct grow space or sealed container for isolation
- Still carries spore-release risk when opening for harvest/maintenance
- Not a sterilization technique -- contamination still progresses, just slowly enough to outrun
- Effectiveness depends on early detection; advanced substrate-wide infection will still overwhelm

**Operational note:** Prioritize quarantine speed over perfection. Even an improvised seal (plastic bag, closet) is better than leaving the tub in the main grow area.

---

## R-02: Thermal Burn Protocol for Surface Trichoderma

**Technique:** Apply butane lighter flame directly to visible trich patches on substrate surface, daily, for 1+ weeks.

**Documented result:** Tub quarantined, visible trich spots burned daily. Tub subsequently pinned and produced a full harvest with only one minor trich return.

**Caveats:**
- Most community advice remains "toss it" -- this is an outlier, not a norm
- Requires quarantine to prevent spore spread to other tubs
- Only viable for early-stage surface contamination, not deep/substrate-wide infection
- Heat may damage mycelium directly -- success depends on precision and timing

---

## R-03: Hydrogen Peroxide Spray for Surface Mold

**Technique:** Light misting of 3% H2O2 on suspicious surface growth.

**Use cases:**
- Second-flush powdery cap coating -- low-risk diagnostic; H2O2 is harmless to fruit body
- Mild cobweb cases (early stage, limited area)

**Limitations:**
- Surface only -- does not penetrate substrate
- Ineffective against bacterial contamination or deep mycelial infection
- Can inhibit pin formation if over-applied

---

## R-04: Fork-Tek / Bubble-Tek for Overlay Recovery

**Technique:** Scratch overlayed substrate surface with fork (fork-tek) or cover with bubble wrap (bubble-tek) to re-expose surface for pinning.

**Use case:** Dense overlay from low FAE (fuzzy feet covering entire surface) prevents pinning.

**Rationale:** Overlay is mycelium, not contamination. Damaging the surface forces re-colonization and new pin sites.

## Operator Error Modes (Not Contamination)

A significant minority of apparent contamination reports (15-20% estimate) describe problems caused by operator technique, not pathogens.

### E-01: Space Heater Environmental Failure

Bald substrate patches + stalled pins + darkening caps caused by localized heat source positioned too close to fruiting chamber. Contamination spreads with defined edges or fuzzy growth; bald spots from heat are sharply demarcated and dry.

**Prevention:** Minimum 1.5m distance between heat source and fruiting chamber. Use ambient room heating rather than directed space heaters.

### E-02: Contamination Fear Leading to Suboptimal Conditions

Excessive caution (sealing tubs, avoiding fanning, maxing humidity) stresses mycelium more than the contamination risk it aims to prevent. Stalled colonization without visible mold improves rapidly when FAE is introduced.

**Key insight:** Moderate FAE reduces contamination risk by preventing stagnant, high-CO2 pockets where anaerobic bacteria thrive. Monotub FAE holes are not optional.

### E-03: Over-Field-Capacity Substrate

Substrate hydrated beyond field capacity creates anaerobic, wet conditions that attract pests and support bacterial bloom.

## Second Flush Risk Profile

Based on analysis of 146 contamination database entries cross-mapped to 46 days of sensor data:

| Risk Factor | First Flush | Second Flush | Ratio |
|-------------|------------|--------------|-------|
| Trichoderma | 1.7% | 15.8% | **9.5x higher** |
| Unknown | 6.7% | 21.1% | **3.2x higher** |
| Powdery coating | 0% | 15.8% | New pattern |
| Bacterial | 20.0% | 5.3% | 0.3x (70% lower) |

**Operational rules:**
1. Trichoderma vigilance must **increase** for second flush, not decrease
2. Powdery coating on second-flush caps is likely spore residue, not contamination
3. Bacterial anxiety is misplaced in second flush; defense shifts to mold
4. Stable humidity prevents the crash-to-Trichoderma escalation pattern

**Sensor-anomaly latency:** Visible contamination reports lag sensor anomalies by 24-48 hours, not same-day. This frames the early-warning detection window.
